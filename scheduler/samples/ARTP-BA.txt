Program Overview
The undergraduate program in Art Practice is designed to help students develop contemporary art practice making skills and think critically about the visual arts and visual culture. Courses focus on the meaning of images and media and their historical development, societal roles, and relationships to disciplines such as literature, music, and philosophy. Work performed in the classroom, studio, and screening room is designed to develop a student’s powers of perception, capacity for visual analysis, and knowledge of technical processes.

Overview and Requirements
The Department of Art & Art History’s year-long Honors Program allows selected Art Practice majors to develop a self-initiated body of thesis artwork throughout their senior year in parallel with their regular work for the Art Practice major.  The body of thesis artwork students propose and develop is expected to deepen and elaborate on work and themes they have begun to explore in previous courses or independent work. This thesis artwork is exhibited in an honors exhibition in the early Spring of the senior year. Students also produce a written thesis paper contextualizing and documenting this work.
The Honors Program is open to Art Practice majors in their senior year who are studying on campus for the entire year. Admission to the Honors Program is decided by the Art Practice faculty via a competitive application process in Spring of the junior year. Completion of the Honors Program is noted on the student’s diploma and transcript.
Participation in the Honors Program requires 10 units of honors-specific coursework dedicated to producing the thesis artwork, exhibition, and written thesis. Up to 4 units of this honors coursework may count towards one of the four upper-level elective courses required for the major. The Honors Program requires a minimum of 71 total credits, an additional 6 units beyond the 65-unit requirement for the Art Practice major.
While Art Practice honors students are expected to work independently to develop artwork for the thesis exhibition, they are supported via ongoing one-on-one tutorials with a faculty thesis mentor (which comprise 3 of the required 10 honors units) and via a year-long honors seminar with other honors students and the Director of the Honors Program (which comprise the remaining 7 required units). In the seminar, students engage with their honors cohort through critiques, visiting artists meetings, and other activities determined by the Honors Director.
During the Honors Program, to support honors students in producing their thesis work, students are typically given access to studio space in a shared honors studio, a materials stipend, and ongoing access to McMurtry Building area-specific studios (photography, sculpture, digital media, printmaking, painting) for which they have received training.

Students are selected for the Honors Program based on the strength of their application portfolio and project proposal. The body of work students propose and develop for an honors exhibition is expected to deepen and elaborate on work and themes they have begun to explore in previous courses or independent work.
Honors Program Requirements
The Art Practice program requires students to develop a sustained studio practice across Fall and Winter Quarters in order to generate a body of thesis artwork approved by their Honors Faculty Mentor and the Honors Director. In the Spring Quarter, this body of thesis work must be successfully installed and exhibited in the thesis exhibition and documented and analyzed through the student’s written thesis document. Students must maintain an A- in all honors course components every quarter to achieve honors on the student’s transcript.

Continuing in honors past Winter Quarter is conditional upon the student’s body of thesis work being ready for the honors exhibition, as evaluated by the Honors Director, the student’s Faculty Mentor, and the Gallery Director.
