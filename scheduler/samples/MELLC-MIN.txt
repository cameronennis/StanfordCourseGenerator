Program Overview
The undergraduate minor in Middle Eastern Languages, Literatures, and Cultures (MELLAC) has been designed to allow students majoring in a variety of departments to gain a substantial introduction to Arabic, Hebrew, Persian, Turkish, Middle Eastern, and African languages, and to the cultures and civilizations of the Middle East and Africa. The minor in Middle Eastern Languages, Literatures, and Cultures (MELLAC) has five tracks.
Declaring the Minor
To declare, students should complete the
MELLAC declaration worksheet
, sending it to the department Faculty Advisor, Alexander Key, for approval. Once signed, they should send the worksheet to the
DLCL Undergraduate Student Services Officer
via email, before completing the final step of declaration via Axess.
Requirements Overview
To earn a minor in Middle Eastern Languages, Literatures, and Cultures, students' plans must adhere to these common overarching requirements:
Complete at least six courses at three units or more and the required overall units of coursework in the department depending on track.
These units may not be used toward any other major or minor.
Courses applied to the minor must be taken for a letter grade, and a C or better must be achieved in each course
Students must test for proficiency in their track language through the Language Center, receiving a notation of “intermediate-high”

Track Requirements
To fulfill the Arabic Track, students must earn a minimum of
24 units total
. The faculty director must approve all courses.
Fulfill ALL of the following requirements:
Required Coursework: Arabic Courses at 3rd, 4th, and 5th-Year Level
Excluding conversation and colloquial courses.
Complete at least 6 of the following Courses:
ARABLANG131 - Third-Year Arabic, First Quarter
ARABLANG132 - Third-Year Arabic, Second Quarter
ARABLANG133 - Third-Year Arabic, Third Quarter
ARABLANG24 - Arabic Skills Workshop
COMPLIT243B - Arabic Poetry
COMPLIT243G - Adab and Ilm: Advanced Readings in Arabic Literature and Science I
AND
Oral Proficiency Interview (OPI)
Students must take the Oral Proficiency Interview (OPI) in Arabic two quarters prior to degree conferral,
minimally earning a notation of "intermediate-high"
