Program Overview
The Neurosciences Interdepartmental Program does not offer a terminal or coterminal MS degree. A MS degree may only be pursued in combination with a doctoral degree from another department within the University or one of the University's professional schools.

Advanced Courses
​​Our program embraces the highly interdisciplinary nature of neuroscience research by allowing students to create an individualized curriculum of advanced topic courses best suited to their research agenda and scientific development needs. This approach has empowered students to take advantage of the breadth of expertise across the university. A minimum of six credits of Advanced Topics coursework is required of each student. Students select the courses while meeting the following criteria: (1) at least one course must be taken with a minimum of three units, (2) the course must have a clear means of evaluation (i.e., problem sets, paper writing, class presentations), and (3) the student must detail how the course relates to the student’s project or domain of neuroscience. This information is submitted in writing along with the course syllabus and examples of problem sets to the co-directors/curriculum committee co-chairs for approval. Six units of advanced neuroscience coursework are required. To complete the six units, at least one course must be three units or more; the remaining units can be completed with a combination of smaller-unit courses (e.g., 3+2+1, 3+2+2, 4+2).
Pre-approval for advanced courses is required.
Required courses must be taken for a letter grade and passed with a 3.0 (B) or better.

Master's Thesis Defense
MS Students will write a proposal in the style of an NIH NRSA proposal and a written rigor document which outlines how the proposed research has addressed NIH's four key areas of rigor as well as a plan for dissemination for research outcomes and products. The student will defend the proposal and rigor document by oral presentation to the Master's thesis committee. The student will complete the proposed Master's thesis project and defend the project through an oral presentation to the Master's thesis committee.
The student must defend the thesis project through oral presentation to the Master's thesis committee by the end of the 2nd year.
