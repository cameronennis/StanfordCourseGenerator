Program Overview
The
Bulletin
outlines the university’s basic requirements for the master’s degree. The Master of Science (AA-MS) degree program in Aeronautics and Astronautics is intended for students whose ultimate goal is to pursue a professional career in Aeronautics and Astronautics, or a related field.
Students with an aeronautical engineering background should be able to complete the master’s degree in five quarters; note that many courses are not taught during the summer. Students with a bachelor’s degree in Physical Science, Mathematics, or other areas of Engineering may find it necessary to take specific prerequisite courses, which may lengthen the time required to obtain the master’s degree.
The M.S. program is a terminal degree program. It is based on completing lectures focused on a theme within Aeronautics and Astronautics engineering. Research is optional (required to take the qualifying examination).
Students who ultimately intend to complete a Ph.D. degree are encouraged to apply directly to the Ph.D. program. Applicants will be able to apply directly to the Ph.D. program whether they hold an M.S. degree or a bachelor’s degree.

Course Requirements
The master’s degree program requires 45 quarter units of coursework, which must be taken at Stanford. All units must be in courses at or above the 100 level.
Students must also meet the university’s quarterly academic requirements for graduate students as described in this Bulletin and in the Degree Completion section of the Guide to Graduate Studies in Aeronautics and Astronautics. All courses (excluding seminars) used to satisfy the requirements for basic courses, mathematics, and technical electives must be taken for a letter grade. Insufficient grade points on which to base the GPA may delay expected degree conferral or result in the refusal of permission to take the qualifying examinations.

Course Waivers
M.S. waiver requests are due by the end of the first week of classes (Autumn Quarter, year 1). Waivers of the basic courses required for the M.S. degree in Aeronautics and Astronautics can only be granted by the instructor of that course. Waivers are applicable only to core courses, which include: AA 210A, AA 200, AA 256, AA 240, Engr 205, AA 203, AA 242A, AA 279A, AA 204, AA 283. Students who believe that they have had a substantially equivalent graduate-level course at another institution should consult with the course instructor to determine if they are eligible for a waiver, and with their advisor to judge the effect on their overall program plans. Students will complete a google form to submit their requests, which will be due by the end of the first week of classes. Examination rooms will be reserved for the second week of the Autumn Quarter.
Students must take the final exam* for the course and achieve a grade of A- or higher. Instructors will forward their waiver approvals to the Student Services Office. One additional technical elective must be added for each M.S. course that is waived. The advisor must sign the front of the program proposal acknowledging the waiver and approving the additional technical elective. *Midterm exam may be used as a substitute if there is no final exam.
Note: This process is distinct from the petition process. Students can still submit petitions for substitutions on the AA-MS program sheet, which will be reviewed by the academic advisor and DGS.

Master’s Degree with Distinction in Research (AA-MS DiR)
A student who wishes to pursue the M.S. in Aeronautics and Astronautics with distinction in research (AA-MS DiR) must first identify a faculty member who agrees to serve as the advisor, supervise the research work, and read the research report. The research advisor must be a member of the Academic Council, supervision by faculty outside of Aero/Astro is permitted. When the research advisor is from outside the Aero/Astro Department, the student must also identify a faculty co-advisor from within the department to provide guidance on departmental requirements and opportunities. The co-advisor must also approve the research report. The faculty commitment may be submitted after the completion of at least on quarter of AA 290 (3 units) and must be submitted prior to the quarter in which the student graduates. In addition, the student must complete the following requirements:
Research Experience
The program should include significant research designated by at least 9 units, and up to 12 units, of independent study (AA 290) over the course of at least 3 academic quarters. A maximum of 12 units may be counted toward the 45 units required for the AA-MS degree. These units may be used to satisfy the technical elective, free elective and/or the experimentation/design requirements of the AA-MS program. All AA 290 units must be completed for letter grades, with a minimum grade of B+ required in each AA 290 course to continue in the program.
Research Report
Students must complete a significant research report detailing their work and its conclusions. The research report represents work that could be publishable in a journal or at a high- quality conference with peer-reviewed proceedings. The student should engage in a discussion with the advisor(s) early in this AA-MS DiR program about the level of contribution and time commitment required to produce the research report. A copy of the research report, with a cover page signed by the advisor (and co-advisor if required) to confirm approval, must be submitted to the AA Student Services Office no later than three weeks before the last day of classes of the quarter in which the student graduates before the distinction-in-research designation can be conferred.
Certificate of Completion
Students who successfully complete the AA-MS DiR program will receive a department certificate of completion and will be listed in the Commencement Program. Please be aware that this will not be reflected on your official transcript or diploma.
