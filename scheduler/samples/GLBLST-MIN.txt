Program Overview
The Global Studies minor is open to Stanford undergraduates from any major and offers an interdisciplinary pathway to explore global issues through one of seven regional specializations. Students engage in in-depth language study while situating their coursework within a broader global context.
Global studies builds on the rich tradition of area studies: the interdisciplinary study of world regions and their intersecting cultures, languages, histories, politics, environments, and societies. By drawing on multiple fields, from human biology and earth sciences to music and engineering, students gain a nuanced understanding of their chosen world region and its place within an interconnected world.
Students from any major who are interested in the Global Studies minor should consult the relevant specialization advisor or the Executive Director of Stanford Global Studies. To declare the minor with one of seven specializations, students must:
Schedule an appointment with the appropriate specialization advisor (see specialization page for contact information)
Declare the Global Studies minor and subplan in Axess
Each student chooses one of seven specializations. The specialization appears on the transcript but does not appear on the diploma.

Subplan: African Studies
Completion requirement
Minor in Global Studies with African Studies Specialization
The Global Studies minor, African Studies specialization, offers students the opportunity to complement their primary course of study with an in-depth, interdisciplinary exploration of Africa's cultures, histories, politics, religions, and societies. Students from any major interested in applying for admission to this minor program should consult the minor advisor at the Center for African Studies. Students declare the minor and the African Studies specialization in Axess (see below for detailed instructions). Students should consult with their minor advisor to develop individual programs. The minor is especially well-suited for undergraduates who plan to do service, research, or study abroad in Africa as part of their Stanford experience.
To declare the Global Studies minor with an African Studies specialization, students must set an appointment with the Associate Director of the Center for African Studies,
Robin Chapdelaine
, then declare the minor and subplan in Axess.
Learning Outcomes
The Global Studies minor specialization in African Studies enables students to develop critical knowledge and skills, organize their interest in Africa into a coherent course of study through directed mentorship and participation in the intellectual community, and prepare for research, study, or service in Africa. Upon completion of the requirements, the Center for African Studies makes the final certification of the minor. The minor and the specialization appear on the transcript but not on the diploma.

Subplan: Islamic Studies

Subplan: Islamic Studies
The minor in  Global Studies with specialization in Islamic Studies offers students an interdisciplinary and global exploration of Islam and Islamicate societies and cultures. The focus is on knowledge of Islam in all its internal complexity, the history of Islam from its beginnings to the 21st century, Islamicate social contexts, and the diversity of human experience as seen in literature and the arts originating in societies affected by Islamic civilizations. Students explore the global extent of Islam and the growth of its diasporas by taking courses on geographical regions such as the Middle East, South Asia, Eurasia, Africa, Western Europe, and the Americas and from disciplines such as anthropology, art and art history, comparative literature, history, political science, international relations, and religious studies.
Students consult with their minor advisor to develop individual programs.
To declare the Global Studies minor with an Islamic Studies specialization, students must set an appointment with the Associate Director for the Islamic Studies Program,
Dr. Halil Yenigun
, then declare the minor and subplan in Axess.
Learning Outcomes
The Global Studies minor specialization in Islamic Studies enables students to:
Recognize, explain, and analyze the beliefs, ideas, practices, histories, ethics, intellectual traditions, cultural and artistic expressions, and politics in Muslim-majority and Muslim minority contexts, including non-Muslim communities.
Apply theoretical concepts, social scientific, or interpretive methods from the humanities and social sciences to design and construct independent research or arguments on any aspect of Islam, Muslims, or non-Muslims in Muslim-majority settings that will prepare them for further pursuit of an advanced degree.
Demonstrate at least an elementary level of proficiency in speaking, comprehending, and writing in one of the native languages spoken in Muslim-majority countries.
Upon completion of the requirements, the final certification of the minor is made by the Abbasi Program in Islamic Studies. The minor and the specialization appear on the transcript but not on the diploma.

Subplan: Latin American Studies
Completion requirement
Subplan: Latin American Studies
The Global Studies minor, Latin American Studies (GS-LAS) specialization, consists of courses surveying the Latin American region’s interdisciplinary fields, including the humanities, social sciences, and environmental and health studies through in-depth coursework and language training. We welcome students from all majors. Those interested in declaring the GS-LAS minor must develop an individual program plan and set up an appointment with a GS-LAS minor advisor (either the CLAS Academic and Student Services Officer or the Associate Director) to review their minor plan PRIOR to declaring in Axess. Units taken for major credit cannot be double-counted towards the minor. The GS-LAS minor is especially well-suited for undergraduates who plan to make service, research, or study abroad in Latin America a part of their Stanford experience. The minor and the specialization appear on the transcript but not on the diploma.
Learning outcomes
The Global Studies minor specialization in Latin American Studies enables students to:
Engage in the integrated understanding of the region’s interdisciplinary fields, including the humanities, social sciences, and environmental and health studies
Gain a conceptual foundation to pursue a variety of career paths in the public and private sectors pertaining to the region
Acquire language and cultural competency

Completion requirement
Subplan: South Asian Studies
The Global Studies minor, South Asian Studies specialization, offers students a focused study from an interdisciplinary perspective of the cultures, histories, politics, religions, and societies of South Asia, including Afghanistan, Bangladesh, Bhutan, India, Maldives, Nepal, Pakistan, and Sri Lanka. The Global Studies Minor with Specialization in South Asian Studies is open to students in any major. Students consult with their minor advisor to develop individual programs.
To declare the Global Studies minor with a South Asian Studies specialization, students must set an appointment with the Associate Director of the Center for South Asia,
Lalita du Perron
, then declare the minor and subplan in Axess.
Learning Outcomes
The Global Studies minor specialization in South Asian Studies enables students to:
Acquire a nuanced and sophisticated understanding of the texts and contexts of South Asian Studies
Work on this geographical and disciplinary area within the broader contours, conversations, and methods of Global Studies
Enhance students’ ability to understand and participate in an increasingly global world
Develop critical and wide-ranging insight into a key world area.
Upon completion of the requirements, the final certification of the minor is made by the Center for South Asian Studies. The minor and the specialization appear on the transcript but not on the diploma.
Additional Course Requirements
At least 25 units of qualifying courses. Students may not double-count courses to complete major and minor requirements. At least 10 of the 25 units must be completed at Stanford.
