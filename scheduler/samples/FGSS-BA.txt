Program Overview
The Program in Feminist, Gender, and Sexuality Studies equips students with the knowledge and skills to examine the significance of gender and sexuality across all areas of human life. Through an interdisciplinary approach, the program explores understandings of and experiences related to gender and sex and how these intersect with race, nationality, religion and belief, class, ethnicity, sexuality, disability, medicine and health, technology, and age. The program also coordinates courses from across the University into a cohesive curriculum in LGBTQ+ studies.
Students design personalized, interdisciplinary programs of study built around a self-defined thematic focus that integrates coursework from multiple departments. Majors may choose one of several subplans—Humanities and the Arts; Technology, Medicine, and Science; Law and Social Sciences; or LGBTQ+ Studies—or may design their own thematic focus. Subplans are printed on the diploma, while individual thematic foci are not. Through this flexible curriculum and opportunities for practical experience, students engage diverse methodologies and theories in gender and sexuality studies, developing the tools to ask critical questions, conduct research, and chart their own intellectual and career paths. The program also offers an interdisciplinary honors program open to undergraduates in all majors.

Capstone Experience
Senior Capstone Project
Students who select to do a senior project for their capstone should enroll in FEMGEN 104X during Spring Quarter of their junior year, followed by FEMGEN 199A in Autumn Quarter of their senior year. Capstone papers must be at least 7,000 words (excluding notes and figures). Creative Capstones may include formats such as short films, podcasts, poetry collections, or performance, and must be paired with a critical written analysis.
All Capstone proposals are reviewed and approved by the FEMGEN 199 instructor(s) and the student’s faculty advisor. Creative projects must meet scholarly standards and clearly articulate their research context, audience, and methodology. Units earned through the FEMGEN 199 series will count toward the major’s unit requirement.

Subplan: Technology, Science, and Medicine
The Technology, Science, and Medicine subplan investigates ethical considerations in how gender, sex, sexuality and other categories of embodied difference shape and are shaped by technology, science, and health and medicine in the application of scientific knowledge.
