Program Overview
Welcome to Design!  The DESIGN-BS bulletin outlines 2026-2027 degree requirements for students pursuing the undergraduate Design major from the
School of Engineering
.  The degree is administered by the
Hasso Plattner Institute of Design at Stanford
(the Stanford d.school).  100% completion of all degree requirements within this engineering program, along with meeting
Undergraduate Academic Progress
, can lead to the conferral of the Bachelor of Science in Design (DESIGN-BS).
Our mission is to graduate designers who can synthesize technology, aesthetics, and business factors in service of the planet and human needs.  Students emerge with a strong technical know-how, and visual thinking abilities that provide human-centered orientation to problem-solving.  The ability to seamlessly integrate across "right brain / left brain" ways of thinking empowers students to make products, services, and experiences with transformative potential.
The d.school is a creative home for the major.  Our students regularly engage in community events, making programs, group work, and the pursuit of meaningful design projects.  This major prepares students for unique internships, research, postgraduate study in design (including Stanford coterminal degrees), public or private sector institutions, entrepreneurial endeavors, and careers that are emerging and may not yet exist.

Breadth in Discipline
Completion requirement
Domain Focus Areas
A Domain Focus Area is a unique opportunity for you to immerse in a field that you are curious about or motivated by.  You can apply your design abilities within a specific context, and emerge having a changed perspective. Each of the domain sphere options is saturated with wicked problems where design work is useful and needed.
You will choose one of four Domain Focus Areas listed below:
[1]
Designing for the Planet
— A domain sphere including Climate, Environment, Oceans, and Energy (and more).  Gain knowledge around environmental problem solving at macro and micro scales, on all corners of our planet.
[2]
Designing for Biological Futures
— A domain sphere including Living Matter and Synthetic Biology (and more)
.
Learn about biofutures and explore the possibilities of building with biology.
[3]
Designing for Health
— A domain sphere including Healthcare & Health Technology Innovation (and more).  Investigate domestic and global governance and their intersection with development, economics, and a more equitable society.
[4]
Designing for Social Impact
— A domain sphere including Global Development, Social Systems, Social Inequity (and more).  Gain understanding in anatomy, physiology, healthcare systems, and health technology while exploring a new era of personalized, accessible, and patient-centered care.
To fulfill a Domain, you will pursue a three-course “pathway”.  Students take 3 classes total: one or more class from [List A], and the remaining classes from [List B]. You should plan your Domain classes and work with the guidance of a d.school Domain Co-Lead.  Chosen domains and associated courses must be on the approved list in the year taken.
