Program Overview
The Center for Russian, East European, and Eurasian Studies (CREEES) one-year interdisciplinary Master of Arts (MA) degree program in Russian, East European, and Eurasian Studies (REEES) provides students with a strong grounding in historical and contemporary processes of change in the Russian Federation, East Europe, the Caucasus, and Central Asia. The program structure provides intellectual cohesion through core requirements while allowing students the flexibility to pursue their particular academic interests with electives and the capstone project, drawing on Stanford’s excellence in teaching and research and rich library and archival resources. The REEES MA program prepares students for a range of professional and academic careers, typically serving students who:
Intend to apply to a PhD program involving Russian, East European, and Eurasian studies and who need to enhance their academic skills and credentials
Intend to pursue careers and/or advanced degrees in such fields as journalism, education, business, government, law, or medicine, and who wish to establish competence in Russian, East European, and Eurasian studies
Mid-career professionals and/or students interested in gaining competence or continuing their interest in Russian, East European, and Eurasian studies

Overview
Candidates for the MA degree must meet university requirements for an MA degree as described in the
Graduate Degrees
section of this Bulletin.
The MA program in REEES ordinarily can be completed in one academic year by a well-prepared student; more extended study periods can be permitted in consultation with the program director. The program can be pursued separately or co-terminally by Stanford undergraduate students.
To meet degree requirements, all students in the REEES MA program must complete a minimum of 48 academic credit units within the guidelines below.

Interdisciplinary and Elective Coursework
Interdisciplinary Elective Course Work:
All courses (other than language courses and approved activity courses) must be taken at the graduate level (200-level or higher); History courses must be taken at the 300-level or higher. Elective courses must be distributed among at least three disciplines (e.g., History, Political Science, Slavic Languages and Cultures, etc.). All coursework applied to the 48-unit minimum must deal primarily with Russian, Eurasian, or East European studies.
For a list of pre-approved courses, see the course list below. In addition, you may view the schedule for pre-approved courses on the
CREEES webpage
, filtering for “MA Pre-approved Courses” for the current academic year. The list of pre-approved courses may be updated as relevant courses are offered. Courses that do not appear on the list may be counted toward the MA through a petition process in consultation with the CREEES associate director if it can be demonstrated that the course is relevant to the MA degree work in REEES topically or methodologically.
Coursework qualifying for the 48-unit minimum must have a letter grade of B or higher (B- does not count for degree credit, nor does CR). Students may apply a maximum of three units of coursework with a final grade of S to the 48-unit minimum. The CREEES associate director must approve S units counting toward the 48-unit minimum. All courses, counting toward the 48-unit minimum, must be approved by the CREEES associate director, who ensures that planned coursework satisfies the requirements for the degree. The CREEES director and steering committee determine the requirements.

Capstone MA Thesis Paper Submission and Presentation
The capstone thesis, a central component of the CREEES MA degree program, is original scholarship produced by students who work on a topic of their choice, under the guidance of a faculty advisor, in consultation with the CREEES director and associate director. Students must enroll in the MA Capstone Seminar REES 300 for one unit in spring quarter, complete a Capstone MA thesis paper, and present the paper during the Capstone Seminar. In addition, student are encouraged to upload the approved thesis to the
Stanford Digital Repository
.
Enroll in the following Courses:
REES300 - MA Capstone Seminar
Enroll in and attend REES 300 in the spring quarter, only.

Language Study Requirement
Students are required to complete at least 1 REEE (Russia, East Europe and Eurasia) area language course per quarter (excluding summer), for a total of 3 courses. Any graduate-level course with a subject code of RUSSLANG;  ARMELANG; CZECLANG; HNGRLANG; POLSHLNG; ROMALANG; or UKRNLANG will count towards the language requirement; other courses may count as well with the consent of the Associate Director or Faculty Director. Additional REEE area language courses can be found through the
Stanford Language Center
website.
These courses must be taken for a letter grade, and students must receive a grade of B or better.
A maximum of 12 units (four units per quarter) from advanced language work is permitted toward the 48-unit degree minimum.
