Program Overview
The Master of Science (MS) in Laboratory Animal Science (MLAS) program in the Department of Comparative Medicine
is a two-year graduate degree designed to prepare students for advanced careers in laboratory animal science, biomedical research, and related scientific fields. The curriculum provides comprehensive training in animal modeling and biomethodology, laboratory animal science, regulatory compliance, animal husbandry and welfare, organizational management, and research facility design.
Students gain a strong academic foundation through interdisciplinary coursework while developing the technical, analytical, and professional skills needed to contribute to the advancement of biomedical research. Graduates are well prepared for careers in academia, biotechnology, pharmaceutical and biomedical industries, and research institutions, or for continued education in medical, veterinary, or other advanced graduate programs.
Advanced Hands-On Learning in Biomedical Research
The MLAS program emphasizes experiential learning by integrating classroom instruction with practical hands-on experiences. Students work alongside leading faculty, researchers, and senior staff, applying scientific principles in real-world laboratory settings while developing critical thinking, problem-solving, and research skills.
NEW FOR 2027: Two Program Pathways
Beginning with the 2027 cohort, the MLAS program offers two distinct academic pathways: a thesis track and a non-thesis track. This allows students to select the experience that best aligns with their career goals and professional interests.
Thesis Track
The traditional
Thesis Track
is designed for students seeking an in-depth research experience. Students are matched with a faculty mentor at the beginning of the program and remain in that laboratory throughout their degree. Working closely with their mentor, students develop and execute an independent research project that culminates in the completion and defense of a master's thesis. This pathway is ideal for students interested in research-intensive careers or pursuing advanced graduate degrees.
Non-Thesis Track
The
Non-Thesis Track
provides a broader experiential learning model for students interested in exploring multiple areas of laboratory animal science and biomedical research. Rather than completing a single, long-term research project, students participate in a series of quarter-long applied projects across a variety of disciplines and settings. Projects may be completed within Stanford laboratories as well as with partnering organizations, providing exposure to diverse research environments, technologies, and professional opportunities. This pathway emphasizes practical experience, professional development, and workforce readiness for careers across academia, industry, and research support organizations. It is ideal for students interested in pursuing advanced professional degrees (including veterinary and medical education and training) or who are exploring careers in research support areas such as regulatory compliance, biomedical research facility operations and management, animal husbandry and welfare, and investigator education and training.
Applicants must indicate their preferred pathway—Thesis Track or Non-Thesis Track—at the time of application.

Requirements Overview
Requirements for all students:
All courses must be taken at the 100 level or above
36 of 45 units must be 200-level or above
All graded courses must achieve a grade of C or higher
Adhere to the university minimum for an overall GPA (3.0)
CompMed 205, 210, 211, 290, and 291 MUST be taken for a letter grade
Students must complete at least 45 units of academic work. All required courses must be completed and will count toward the 45-unit minimum.
Thesis Track:
The remaining units needed to meet the 45-unit minimum can be fulfilled with research credit (COMPMED 399 - Graduate research) or electives, as approved by each student’s research mentor.
Non Thesis Track:
In addition to the required courses (listed below), students should enroll in COMPMED 260 for rotation credit. Out-of-department electives should be approved by the department's Student Services Officer before enrolling.
Students will also participate in 3-4 quarters of hands-on rotations, for credit. Hands-on rotations will be done both within and outside of Stanford University. Instead of a final thesis, trainees in the non-thesis track will produce deliverables for each rotation, including (but not limited to) generation of standard operating procedures (SOP), reports on findings, recommendations and implementation guidelines, educational modules, etc. The type of deliverable for each rotation will be determined by each rotation mentor(s).
Completion Timeline
Per university policy, the master’s degree must be completed within three years.

Research Requirement
Completion requirement
Thesis
Students must complete a master’s thesis, which may take the following form:
Original analysis of original data
A comprehensive literature review with a meta-analysis of data or a critical reanalysis of data
Evaluation of a methodological problem using actual data
A comprehensive literature review with a grant proposal (NIH style format) for a new study to bridge a gap in the existing knowledge.
Students must present their research  to the department.
While not required, students are encouraged to publish a research paper that would meet the thesis requirement.
Non Thesis Option:
Hands-on rotations will be done both within and outside of Stanford University. Instead of a final thesis, trainees in the non-thesis track will produce deliverables for each rotation, including (but not limited to) generation of standard operating procedures (SOP), reports on findings, recommendations and implementation guidelines, educational modules, etc.
