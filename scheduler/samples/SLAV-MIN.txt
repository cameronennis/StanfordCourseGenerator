Program Overview
The mission of the undergraduate program in Slavic Languages and Literatures is to expose students to various perspectives on Slavic, especially Russian language, history, culture, literature, and philosophical thought. The program offers two tracks. The Russian Language subplan prepares students’ oral and written proficiency in Russian to communicate effectively beyond the interpersonal level. Courses in the Russian Language and Literature subplan focus on the linguistic and philological study of literature and the history of Russian literature.
Declaring the Minor
To declare, students should complete the
Slavic Languages and Literatures declaration worksheet
, sending it to the department Director of Undergraduate Studies (DUS) for approval. Once signed, they should send the worksheet to the
DLCL Undergraduate Student Services Officer
via email, before completing the final step of declaration via Axess.
Requirements Overview
To earn a minor in Slavic Languages and Literatures, students' plans must adhere to these common overarching requirements:
Complete at least six courses at three units or more and 24 overall units of coursework in the department.
These 24 units may not be used toward any other major or minor.
Courses applied to the minor must be taken for a letter grade.

Preparing for the Minor
Students interested in
Slavic Languages and Literatures Russian Language subplan
should begin by taking first-year or taking the Language Center placement examination for a Slavic language to demonstrate their fluency. First-Year language coursework (RUSSLANG 1, 2, & 3, or 1A & 2A, or equivalent) are prerequisites, and will not count toward the minor units requirement.
Early in their studies, it is recommended that students take introductory courses in literature, language, or the humanities to build a foundation in literary traditions, theory, and languages.
To plan their course of study, including electives and any thematic or regional focus, students should consult the department's Program Worksheet, available from the
DLCL Undergraduate Student Services Officer
, and discuss it with the Director of Undergraduate Studies (DUS).
