Program Overview
Welcome to Design!  The DESIGN-MS bulletin outlines 2026-2027 degree requirements for students pursuing the Master of Science degree (MS) from the
School of Engineering
.  The master's degree is only offered to active students admitted through
Graduate Admissions
.  The degree is administered by the
Hasso Plattner Institute of Design at Stanford
(The Stanford d.school).  100% completion of all degree requirements within this engineering program, along with meeting
Graduate Academic Progress
, can lead to the conferral of the Master of Science in Design (DESIGN-MS).
Master of Science (MS) Design students (nicknamed Loftees) will develop a strong practice in human-centered design abilities, making, creativity, and applied project work in their chosen domain.  The first year foregrounds a structured design practice that pushes to stretch intellectually and grow their skills as makers across mediums.  Students will advance in focus through choosing one method and identify a domain focus.  A Method will allow students to gain a depth of practice in a select set of mediums.  The Domain area will allow exploring challenges in different disciplines where designers can have an impact, and where there are increasing academic and professional opportunities.
The degree prepares students for further study in design, design careers in public or private sector institutions, entrepreneurial endeavors, and even careers that may not yet exist.

A Methods Depth lets you take classes across the university to sharpen making skills, abilities, and gain professional fluency.  Units for each Method should range from 8-10 units.  Within each method you will take three classes with a focus on foundational understanding and application. You should decide on your Method depth no later than Winter quarter of your first year of graduate studies.  You must choose one of three Method Depths tracks listed below.
[1] Physical Design + Manufacturing
— Methods range from classes on materials and mechanisms (to understand what is possible with physical design) to manufacturing processes and supply chain logistics (to understand how physical design is implemented).
[2] Emerging Technologies + Digital User Experience
— Methods range from foundational programming methodologies and human-computer interaction classes to applied courses on digital prototyping, user experience design, and incorporating AI and data science.
[3] Human Behavior + Multi-stakeholder Research
— Methods span qualitative and quantitative approaches to better understand people – who we are, what we do, and why we do it.  Classes focus on communication, data science, and psychology to help understand how to design for the human experience.

Domain Focus Area
A Domain Focus Area is a unique opportunity for you to immerse in a field that you are curious about or motivated by.  You can apply their design abilities within a specific context, and emerge having a changed perspective. Domain courses give you a breadth and depth understanding of a context where design work might be applied.
To fulfill a Domain, you will identify the name of your focus and pursue a rigorous course load that is pre-planned and coordinated with your faculty advisor.  Units are expected to be a minimum of 8 units, and all courses must have alignment with the scope of your Domain.
Note
:
After your Domain is defined and all classes coordinated with your faculty advisor, the Design Student Services Officer will setup your My Academic Path (MAP) page to account for those classes in the Domain section.
