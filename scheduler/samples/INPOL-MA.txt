Program Overview
The Ford Dorsey Master’s in International Policy (MIP) is an interdisciplinary program devoted to rigorously analyzing international policy issues in diplomacy, governance, cyber and international security, and environmental policy. The two-year, full-time degree program awards the Master of Arts (MA) in International Policy.
Unique for small, collaborative classes led by top Stanford faculty and seasoned practitioners, MIP’s core curriculum emphasizes political science, economics, and international relations with quantitative methods and a case study approach. The curriculum includes a strong practical component, customization, and several subplans corresponding to expertise at the
Freeman Spogli Institute for International Studies at Stanford (FSI).
In keeping with the university’s mission, MIP was established in 1982 to prepare the next generation of leaders for public and private sector careers in international policy-making and implementation. Its curriculum effectively draws upon FSI’s world-class faculty and interdisciplinary culture, breadth of teaching across theory and practice, multidisciplinary research capacity, and Stanford and Silicon Valley’s ethos of innovation. The program offers its master’s students a unique education to examine, understand, and solve complex real-world problems.

Customized Electives
Students must complete a minimum of
24 units
of Customized Electives.
Customized Electives provide students the opportunity to engage with academic offerings across the University and tailor their course of study to their individual academic and professional interests. All courses applied toward this requirement must be broadly related to international policy.
Students may fulfill this requirement through a variety of pathways, including but not limited to: enrollment in additional subplan courses; completion of certificate programs (e.g., Computational Social Science Certificate); enrollment in language courses; completion of coursework in a second subplan (informally); or a combination of courses across disciplines that support the student’s academic objectives.
Courses numbered below 100 may not be applied toward graduate degree requirements, in accordance with University policy. In addition, activity courses, as well as performance and visual arts courses, do not satisfy MIP degree requirements.

Subplan Course Petition Process
Twice per year, MIP accepts petitions to add courses to the list of approved subplan electives. This process allows students to propose courses for review by MIP leadership and faculty subject-matter leads for the relevant subplan.
Petition submission timelines will be announced in advance of each review cycle. Students must submit complete course information, including a syllabus and a written rationale for inclusion.
Course review criteria include:
Alignment with subplan learning objectives
Student-provided rationale for inclusion
Recency and consistency of course offerings
Graduate-level designation (generally courses numbered 200 and above)
Availability of a letter grade option (except in GSB, Law, and School of Medicine courses where applicable)
Teaching evaluations, when available
Skills-based or primarily experiential courses are typically classified as
Customized Electives
rather than subplan electives.
Approved courses will be added to the official subplan elective list. Students may apply a course retroactively toward subplan requirements if it is approved after the course has been completed.
The elective course list includes both currently offered and historically approved courses, maintained as part of the program record in accordance with University policy. A current list of active courses is available on the
MIP website
.

Stanford MIP-Vienna School of International Studies Academic Exchange
The Stanford MIP–Vienna School of International Studies Academic Exchange is a one-quarter (Autumn Quarter) exchange program between the Ford Dorsey Master’s in International Policy (MIP) and the Diplomatische Akademie Wien – Vienna School of International Studies (DA). Each year, two second-year students from each institution are selected through a competitive application process to participate in the exchange. Selected students receive fellowships to study at the partner institution, where they enroll as full-time students, participate in academic and extracurricular activities, and engage in the intellectual community of the host institution.
Academic Policies
Students participating in the exchange must complete all requirements of the MIP MA curriculum. The minimum number of Stanford units required for graduation is reduced to
65 units
for students participating in the exchange.
In addition to the Stanford unit requirement, students must complete the equivalent of at least
15 units (typically four or more full-time courses)
at the Vienna School of International Studies.  These courses will apply towards the subplan electives or Customized Electives.  As such, the two required subplan courses must be completed at Stanford.
MIP exchange participants must submit their proposed DA course selections to the MIP Academic Services Team for approval no later than the
end of the first week of classes in the Autumn Trimester at the DA
.
Students are strongly encouraged to carefully evaluate how coursework completed at the DA will apply toward MIP degree requirements and to consider the implications of spending one quarter away from Stanford.
Enrollment and Transcript Policy
While participating in the exchange, students remain active in their Stanford academic program; however, they are not considered enrolled at Stanford during the exchange quarter. Students receive an official transcript from the Vienna School of International Studies for the Autumn Trimester. The exchange is not reflected on the Stanford transcript.
Additional Information
Further details are available on the
Stanford–Vienna Academic Exchange
page of the MIP website.

The dual MBA/MA program is typically completed in approximately
three academic years
, depending on prior academic preparation and quantitative background.
Admissions decisions are made independently by each program. Units may not be double-counted toward both degrees; coursework must be applied to either the MBA or the MA degree requirements, but not both.
Students in the dual degree program must complete:
90 units
for the MBA
54 units
for the MA in International Policy, including the following requirements:
Core curriculum: 27 units
Subplan: 18 units
Capstone: 9 units
Students should consult both the MBA Program Office and the MIP Assistant Director for Academic and Student Affairs for additional guidance.

Students must complete all degree requirements for both programs, including
80 units
for the MA in International Policy and
45 units
for the MS in Environment and Resources. Completing the MS degree typically requires at least three additional quarters of study beyond the time required to complete the MA.
Joint Degree: LAW-JD/INPOL-MA
The joint JD requires 111 units, and the MA in International Policy requires 54 units. Students enrolled in the joint program must complete the following MIP requirements:
Core curriculum: 27 units
Subplan 18 units
Capstone 9 units
Students who begin their studies at Stanford Law School may, with MIP approval, complete the required statistics sequence through the Law School in place of the INTLPOL statistics sequence. However, this option is not recommended, as the Law School sequence typically includes a single statistics course, whereas the INTLPOL sequence consists of two courses that provide broader methodological preparation (including training in R) for the MIP capstone.
Students who elect this option may be at a disadvantage in capstone preparation. Prior approval from MIP is required. Any resulting unit adjustments will be applied to subplan requirements as appropriate.
In addition,
Customized Electives
are not required for joint JD students who matriculate first in the Law School. In some cases, Customized Electives may also be waived for students who matriculate first in MIP, subject to program approval.
